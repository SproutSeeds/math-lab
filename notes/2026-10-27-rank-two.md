# Rank-two PPT states in Lean

Date: 27 October 2026. Labels: Exact / Verified / Heuristic / Conjecture (ORP).

## Result (Verified)

`ρ_A` and `ρ_B` are the reduced operators (`ChainReduction.redA`, `ChainReduction.redB`).

- `ChainReduction.sep_of_rank_le_two`. For all n and m, a PPT operator on ℂⁿ ⊗ ℂᵐ of rank at most two is
  separable (P. Horodecki, Smolin, Terhal and Thapliyal 2003).
- `ChainReduction.reduction_criterion`. Every PPT operator satisfies `ρ ⪯ ρ_A ⊗ 1`, in every dimension
  (the reduction criterion of M. and P. Horodecki 1999, and of Cerf, Adami and Gingrich 1999).
- `ChainReduction.rank_redA_le` and `ChainReduction.rank_redB_le`. A PPT operator has rank at least that
  of each reduced operator.
- `ChainReduction.bell_not_ppt`. The PPT hypothesis is needed. The Bell state has rank one and
  `ρ_A = 1` of rank two, so it is not PPT, and by Peres's criterion it is not separable.
- Two general tools:
  - `exists_sum_rank_one`: a positive semidefinite matrix is a sum of `rank` rank-one terms.
  - `exists_support`: an isometry W onto the support of a positive semidefinite matrix X, with
    `X (1 − W Wᴴ) = 0` and `Wᴴ X W` invertible.

Definitions:

- `redA ρ = tr_B ρ` and `redB ρ = tr_A ρ`;
- rank is Mathlib's `Matrix.rank`;
- `IsPPT` and `IsSeparable` are those of `Duality.lean`, unchanged.

None of these statements is vacuous:

- `bell_not_ppt` shows that the rank inequality, and with it the theorem's route, fails without PPT.
- PPT entangled states exist at higher rank, for example Horodecki's 2 × 4 state of rank 5
  (`ChainLowRank.rho24M_sharp`).

## The proof, file by file

All steps are in `ChainTheory/Reduction.lean`.

| Step | Statement | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The reduction identity `2 (ρ_A ⊗ 1 − ρ) = Σ_pq (1 ⊗ A_pq) ρ^Γ (1 ⊗ A_pq)ᴴ`, with the antisymmetric units `A_pq = E_pq − E_qp`. So a PPT operator satisfies `ρ ⪯ ρ_A ⊗ 1` | `reduction_identity`, `reduction_criterion` |
| 2 | A positive semidefinite matrix is a sum of `rank` rank-one terms, from Mathlib's spectral theorem | `exists_sum_rank_one` |
| 3 | If `ρ ⪯ ρ_A ⊗ 1` and `ρ_A` is invertible, then `n ≤ rank ρ`. Details below | `le_rank_of_reduction` |
| 4 | Compress ℂⁿ to the support of ρ_A, where ρ_A is invertible. This gives `rank ρ_A ≤ rank ρ`; swapping the factors gives the same for ρ_B | `exists_support`, `rank_redA_le`, `rank_redB_le` |
| 5 | ρ lives on (support of ρ_A) ⊗ (support of ρ_B): if `ρ_A K = 0` then `ρ (K ⊗ 1) = 0`, and likewise on the other factor | `mul_kron_one_eq_zero`, `mul_one_kron_eq_zero` |
| 6 | Rank two. Details below | `sep_of_rank_le_two` |

Step 3 runs as follows.

- Write `ρ = Σᵢ ψᵢ ψᵢ†` with r = rank ρ terms, and set `K = ρ_A⁻¹ ⊗ 1`.
- The Gram matrix `Gᵢⱼ = ψᵢ† K ψⱼ` has trace n, because `tr(K ρ) = tr(ρ_A⁻¹ ρ_A)`.
- The criterion applied at `K ψⱼ` gives `Σᵢ |Gᵢⱼ|² ≤ Gⱼⱼ`.
- Cauchy–Schwarz then gives `n² ≤ r · Σⱼ Gⱼⱼ² ≤ r n`, so n ≤ r.

Step 6 runs as follows.

- Both supports have dimension at most two.
- Compress ρ to the two supports and embed the result in ℂ² ⊗ ℂ².
- Apply the two-qubit theorem: Physical Superintelligence's, through `Stormer.lean`.

## Where it departs from the paper

- **The rank inequality.** Horodecki, Smolin, Terhal and Thapliyal show that if `rank ρ` is less than the
  rank of ρ_A or of ρ_B, then ρ violates the reduction criterion and is distillable. Here the criterion
  gives the inequality directly, by the Gram matrix and Cauchy–Schwarz, with no bound on eigenvalues.
- **The criterion.** It comes from an explicit Kraus identity for the reduction map, not from citing that
  the map is decomposable.
- **Supports.** They are spans of eigenvectors with nonzero eigenvalues, from Mathlib's spectral theorem.

## Limits

- **Rank three.** PPT states of rank three are separable as well (Horodecki, Lewenstein, Vidal and Cirac
  2000), but that is not formalized here.
  - When one reduced operator has rank at most two, it follows from these tools and the 2 × m theorem
    (`LowRank.lean`).
  - The remaining case, where ρ, ρ_A and ρ_B all have rank three, needs the M × N low-rank theorem.
- **Rank four.** PPT entangled states of rank four exist, from unextendible product bases (Bennett et al.
  1999). They are not formalized here.
