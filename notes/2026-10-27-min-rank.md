# The minimal rank of PPT entanglement in Lean

Date: 27 October 2026. Labels: Exact / Verified / Heuristic / Conjecture (ORP).

## Result (Verified)

`ρ_A` and `ρ_B` are the reduced operators (`ChainReduction.redA`, `ChainReduction.redB`).

- `ChainLowRankMN.sep_of_rank_le_rank_redB`. For all n and m, a PPT operator on ℂⁿ ⊗ ℂᵐ whose rank is at
  most that of ρ_B is separable (P. Horodecki, Lewenstein, Vidal and Cirac 2000).
- `ChainLowRankMN.sep_of_rank_le_rank_redA` and `ChainLowRankMN.sep_of_rank_le_max`. The same with ρ_A,
  and the usual form `rank ρ ≤ max(rank ρ_A, rank ρ_B)`.
- `ChainLowRankMN.sep_of_rank_le_three`. Every PPT operator of rank at most three is separable, in every
  dimension (Horodecki, Lewenstein, Vidal and Cirac 2000).
- `ChainLowRankMN.min_rank_ppt_entangled`. **The minimal rank of PPT entanglement is four.** Every PPT
  operator of rank at most three is separable, and the tiles state on ℂ³ ⊗ ℂ³ is PPT, is not separable,
  and has rank four (`ChainTiles.tiles_ppt_entangled`).
- `ChainLowRankMN.four_le_rank`. Every PPT operator that is not separable has rank at least four.
- Two general tools, in `Positivity/NormalFamily.lean`:
  - `MathLab.fuglede`: if N is normal and A commutes with N, then A commutes with Nᴴ (Fuglede's theorem
    for matrices).
  - `MathLab.normal_spectral_family`: a family of matrices that commute with each other and with each
    other's adjoints is diagonalized by one unitary.

A PPT operator has rank at least that of each reduced operator (`rank_redA_le`, `rank_redB_le`). So the
hypothesis `rank ρ ≤ max(rank ρ_A, rank ρ_B)` means equality.

None of these statements is vacuous, and the bounds are sharp:

- **PPT is needed.** The Bell state `|Φ⟩⟨Φ|` is a projector of rank one, and it is neither PPT nor
  separable (`ChainReduction.bell_not_ppt`).
- **The rank condition cannot be relaxed by one.** P. Horodecki's 2 × 4 state is PPT and entangled, with
  rank 5 and ρ_B of rank 4 (`ChainLowRank.rho24M_sharp`).
- **Three is the largest separable rank.** The tiles state has rank four.

## The proof

All steps are in `ChainTheory/LowRankMN.lean` unless the table says otherwise. Write `X(r) = Σ_ab r_a ρ_ab
conj(r_b)` for the compression of ρ to a row `r ∈ ℂⁿ`, an m × m matrix.

| Step | Statement | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Two-row shear. For PPT ρ and rows r, s, some real z makes every kernel vector of `X(r + z s)` a kernel vector of `X(r)` and of `X(s)`. This is the 2 × m shear applied to the compression of ρ to two rows | `exists_shear_ker` (`Woronowicz.lean`), `exists_shear_rows` |
| 2 | Shearing in one row at a time gives a row r with `r₀ = 1` and `ker X(r) ⊆ ker ρ_B`, since `ρ_B = Σ_a X(e_a)` | `exists_row` |
| 3 | Normal form. Details below | `exists_normal_form` |
| 4 | Gram form. Details below | `blkAt_eq_gram` |
| 5 | Normality. Details below | `normal_of_ppt_corner`, `normal_comb` |
| 6 | Commutation. Normality of `B_a`, `B_b`, `B_a + B_b` and `B_a + i B_b` gives `B_aᴴ B_b = B_b B_aᴴ` | `comm_of_normal`, `comm_blocks` |
| 7 | Separability. Details below | `sep_of_gram_comm` |
| 8 | The general case. Details below | `sep_of_rank_le_rank_redB`, `sep_of_rank_le_rank_redA` |
| 9 | Rank three. If rank ρ is at most the rank of ρ_A or of ρ_B, step 8 applies. Otherwise both reduced operators have rank at most two, so ρ lives on a 2 × 2 space, where PPT operators are separable | `sep_of_rank_le_three`, `sep_of_ranks_le_two` (`Reduction.lean`) |
| 10 | Minimal rank. Step 9 and the tiles state | `min_rank_ppt_entangled` |

Step 3 runs as follows. Suppose ρ_B is invertible.

- By step 2, `X(r)` is invertible. Write it as `Fᴴ F`.
- Apply the row operation whose first row is r and whose other rows are those of the identity. Then apply
  `1 ⊗ (Fᴴ)⁻¹`. The `(0, 0)` block becomes 1.
- Both operations are invertible, so ρ is separable if the result is.

Step 4 runs as follows. Suppose the `(0, 0)` block is 1 and rank ρ ≤ m.

- The m columns through the `(0, 0)` block are linearly independent.
- So they span the column space.
- Reading off coefficients in the first block row gives `ρ_ab = B_aᴴ B_b`, where `B_a` is the `(0, a)`
  block.

Step 5 runs as follows.

- For any row s, compress ρ to the rows `e₀` and s. This gives the 2 × m corner form
  `[[1, N], [Nᴴ, Nᴴ N]]` with `N = Σ_b conj(s_b) B_b`.
- The compression is PPT. PPT gives `Nᴴ N ⪰ N Nᴴ`, and the difference has trace zero, so N is normal.
- So every combination `Σ_b c_b B_b` is normal.

Step 7 runs as follows.

- Fuglede's theorem turns `B_aᴴ B_b = B_b B_aᴴ` into `B_a B_b = B_b B_a`.
- The `B_a` diagonalize together as `B_a = U diag(d_a) Uᴴ`.
- Then `ρ = Σ_p (x_p ⊗ u_p)(x_p ⊗ u_p)†`, where `(x_p)_a = conj(d_a(p))` and `u_p` is the p-th column of U.

Step 8 runs as follows.

- Compress ℂᵐ to the support of ρ_B (`exists_support`). There ρ_B is invertible, and ρ lives on that
  support (`mul_one_kron_eq_zero`). Steps 3 to 7 then apply.
- Swapping the two factors gives the ρ_A form.

## Choices made here

- **The normal form.** The invertible row comes from an explicit shear on two rows at a time and induction
  over the rows. No genericity argument is used.
- **Normality.** Each combination of the blocks is checked on a 2 × m compression. This reuses the corner
  argument of `LowRank.lean`.
- **Commutation.** Polarization with the coefficients 1 and i turns normality of combinations into the
  commutation relations.
- **Fuglede's theorem.** It is proved for matrices from the spectral theorem for normal matrices.

## Limits

- **The decomposition.** It is not shown to be unique. The number of product terms is not stated in the
  main theorem.
- **Other results of the paper.** Horodecki, Lewenstein, Vidal and Cirac also give an algorithm to find the
  product vectors, and treat states beyond the rank condition. Neither is formalized here.
- **Rank-four examples.** The tiles state on ℂ³ ⊗ ℂ³ is the only one stated.
